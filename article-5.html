Sleep

Why you wake at 3am

It's not insomnia. It's your body sending a signal — and most people misread it.

3 min read · Updated Nov 2026

You fall asleep fine. Then, like clockwork — 3am. Eyes open. Mind running.

You'd call it insomnia. Your body is actually trying to tell you something specific.

3am is a chemical event, not a coincidence

Around 3–4am, cortisol — the stress hormone — begins its pre-dawn rise. If your daytime stress is already high, that rise overshoots and jolts you awake. At the same time, if your blood sugar dipped overnight, your body releases adrenaline to compensate. Two quiet drivers, one wake-up.

The two most common causes

  • A late spike, then a crash — dinner heavy in carbs or alcohol pushes glucose up, then it falls off a cliff at 3am.
  • An over-amped stress system — a cortisol baseline that's been running hot all day.

What actually helps (in order)

  • Fix the last meal — protein and vegetables, carbs earlier in the day, alcohol to a minimum.
  • Down-regulate before bed — the ten-minute extended-exhale breathing from the second article.
  • Same wake time, seven days a week — it anchors the cortisol rhythm.

Don't fight the wake-up with screen time or a 3am snack — both train your body to repeat it.
